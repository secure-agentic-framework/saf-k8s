# SAF-K8S-1003-002 — OCTAVE assessment traceability

```sh
python3 lab.py test SAF-K8S-1003-002 --docker-context desktop-linux
```

From the framework checkout, use `python3 tests/control-lab/lab.py` with the same arguments. Three native Kubernetes Jobs on v1.36.1 Kind test the worksheet in a digest-pinned Python container. The disabled workflow accepts an incomplete record. The enabled gate rejects missing asset impacts, infrastructure links, tolerance, treatment and a stale review date. It accepts a complete record with four synthetic asset classes and mounted evidence references.

Start an assessment with `template.json`. Record asset-based threats, review supporting infrastructure, and link treatment decisions to recorded impacts and tolerance. Keep the review date, change triggers, evidence references and accountable owner in the worksheet. Place referenced artifacts at paths relative to the worksheet.

An organizational assessment needs actual workshops, business-impact judgment, authorized tolerance, risk-treatment decisions and evidence from deployed controls. The included worksheet illustrates traceability and leaves those judgments to the assessment team.

## Scratch additive freshness candidate

The candidate retains the three published Jobs and adds one independently observed
case-owned topology ConfigMap, plus five review Jobs. A prior review is accepted against its captured ConfigMap UID, governed revision
annotation and content digest. A metadata-only annotation update advances
resourceVersion but leaves the same review valid. The fixture then changes the same
ConfigMap's serving/GPU topology and governed revision, expects the old review to
fail, and accepts a refreshed worksheet bound to the new semantic state. Raw scoped
ConfigMap data and version metadata are retained so reviewers can recompute the digest.
Each selected review Pod is verified against its controller Job UID. No live platform, real assets or organizational
OCTAVE conclusions are represented.

The added date cases use fixed authoritative `last_review` values and a deterministic
calendar policy: a February 29 review is due February 28 in a non-leap year. They
check the due date itself, the following overdue day, and rejection of dates later
than the one-year anniversary. This tests worksheet freshness mechanics only; the
organization still chooses its approved cadence and must redo its actual assessment
after major changes.

## Phase 3 evaluation and treatment traceability

The Phase 3 case runs seven native Jobs over immutable synthetic worksheets.
The valid worksheet records a declared criterion and outcome for each threat
and links treatment priority and rationale to that same evaluation. The other
Jobs compare missing evaluations, dangling criteria, missing outcomes,
cross-asset treatment links, misbound priorities, and duplicate scenarios in
one asset. Raw evidence ties the worksheets to their ConfigMap digests and
the exact Job-owned Pods.

Use organization-approved criteria and outcomes in `template.json`. Record
each critical asset and its security requirements, business impact, threat
evaluation, and supporting infrastructure. Link treatments and priority
rationales to the evaluated threats and the authorized tolerance source. Retain
the analysis team's results and management's decisions. Repeat the review when
governed major changes occur or the annual review is due. The organization owns
the ratings, treatment priorities, and approval records.
