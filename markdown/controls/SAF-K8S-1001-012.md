# SAF-K8S-1001-012 - SIEM correlation rules for AI-workload anomaly indicators

- Domain: D10 - Observability, Incident Response, and Governance
- Knowledge Area: 10.1 - Logging and Audit
- Maturity: Practitioner
- Classification: ai-specific

## Statement

Organizations must develop and maintain SIEM correlation rules for defined AI-workload security indicators and policy violations, including unexpected model-service changes, GPU utilization inconsistent with an approved workload, training-job changes outside authorized ownership, and inference-service request-rate deviations. Treat a rule match as an alert for investigation, not as proof of an attack or compromise.

## Objective

Detect and route candidate AI-workload security anomalies with enough source, identity, workload, and timing context for analyst validation. Evaluate rule coverage, input availability, alert delivery, and false-positive and false-negative behavior in the deployed monitoring system.

## Rationale

Generic infrastructure rules may lack context needed to interpret AI-workload changes. Sustained GPU utilization without a matching training job, an unexpected model-service change, an unusual request-rate deviation, or large model-storage egress can be useful investigation indicators, but each can also have an authorized or operational explanation. Correlation logic should bind the signal to its actual source, identity, workload owner, approved activity, threshold, and time window. A match is a candidate alert; an analyst validates authorization, context, and corroborating evidence before classifying an incident. A ConfigMap update is evidence of a Kubernetes object change only. It establishes a change to the active kube-apiserver audit policy only when that ConfigMap is an actual input to the control-plane configuration and the effective configuration and resulting audit behavior are verified. Signals must come from sources onboarded under SAF-K8S-1001-028, with ingestion health and delivery completeness addressed under SAF-K8S-1001-029.

## Scope

Applies to all production Kubernetes clusters hosting AI workloads. Covers SIEM correlation rule sets for AI-specific attack detection scenarios. Depends on SIEM source onboarding and forwarding (SAF-K8S-1001-028) and ongoing ingestion health and delivery completeness monitoring (SAF-K8S-1001-029).

## Enforcement

- Primary Enforcement Point: Correlation rule engine management within the SIEM platform.
- Implementation Surface: Candidate correlation rules covering: model-service CREATE or UPDATE by an identity outside the approved ownership mapping; GPU utilization inconsistent with the declared training or inference workload and approved operating window; training-job PATCH or UPDATE by an identity outside its approved owner set; inference request-rate deviation from a documented baseline; model-storage egress above an owner-approved threshold and window; and changes to the deployed audit-policy source or effective kube-apiserver audit configuration. For each rule, record the data source and version, identity and workload joins, threshold/window, approved suppression, alert destination, and analyst disposition. A ConfigMap audit signal must be named as a configuration-change indicator unless effective control-plane consumption and resulting audit behavior are separately observed.
- Owner Primary: Detection Engineering
- Owner Supporting: Security Operations; AI/ML Engineering; Platform Engineering
