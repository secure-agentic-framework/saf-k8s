# SAF-K8S-0901-007 - AI operator privilege management

- Domain: D09 - AI Workload Security: Training, Serving, and Pipelines
- Knowledge Area: 9.1 - Distributed Training Workload Security
- Maturity: Practitioner
- Classification: ai-specific

## Statement

AI-specific Kubernetes operators (including NVIDIA GPU Operator, KServe, Kubeflow, Ray, Volcano, and Kueue) must use dedicated service accounts and least-privilege RBAC. Constrain each controller’s watch and tenant access to the narrowest scope supported by its documented operating mode; explicitly approve, minimize, and document necessary cluster-scoped permissions and resources. Maintain a defined vulnerability-management and upgrade cadence for each deployed operator and its dependencies.

## Objective

Reduce the resources and namespaces reachable after an AI operator compromise by applying least-privilege RBAC and the narrowest supported watch and tenant scope, explicitly reviewing necessary cluster-scoped access, and maintaining a vendor- and version-aware vulnerability and upgrade process.

## Rationale

AI operators differ in the resources and scope required by their functions and deployment modes. Some operate across namespaces, nodes, or cluster-scoped APIs, while others support a namespace-scoped mode. A namespace-only rule is not appropriate for every product; permissions should follow the documented mode and be minimized, reviewed, and explicitly approved at the scope required. Because operator images, dependencies, CRDs, drivers, and APIs have version-specific support and security status, vulnerability and upgrade decisions must follow the deployed vendor release and platform compatibility guidance.

## Scope

Applies to deployed AI-specific Kubernetes operators, including NVIDIA GPU Operator, KServe, Kubeflow, Ray, Volcano, and Kueue. Covers each deployed version and operating mode; its service accounts, Roles and ClusterRoles, bindings, watch and tenant scope, cluster-scoped resources and permissions, operator images and dependencies, and documented vulnerability and upgrade process. Namespace restrictions apply where supported; required cluster-scoped access must be minimized, justified, and approved. Excludes general-purpose operators not specific to AI workloads.

## Enforcement

- Primary Enforcement Point: Operator deployment and version manifests, service-account and RBAC resources, controller watch and tenant-scope configuration, documented approval for cluster-scoped grants, and vendor-specific vulnerability and upgrade review.
- Implementation Surface: Pinned vendor release and mode; dedicated service accounts; least-privilege Roles and ClusterRoles and bindings; narrowest supported controller watch and tenant scope; minimized and approved cluster-scoped grants; CRD and node access review; operator image and dependency vulnerability scanning; vendor advisory and compatibility review; and documented upgrade, validation, and rollback cadence.
- Owner Primary: ML Platform Engineering
- Owner Supporting: Platform Engineering; Security Engineering
